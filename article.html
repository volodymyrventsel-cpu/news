Federal Reserve Holds Rates Steady as Inflation Cools to 2.4%

May 8, 2026 7 min read By Brennan Whitfield

The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate in the 4.00–4.25% range on Wednesday, the third consecutive meeting without a move, as the central bank said it would wait for more evidence that the cooling in inflation is durable before cutting again.

Headline CPI fell to 2.4% in April, the lowest reading since early 2021 and the second straight month within striking distance of the Fed's 2% target. Core inflation — the measure that strips out food and energy — eased to 2.7%, with services inflation continuing the slow descent that has frustrated policymakers for the better part of two years.

Markets, which had priced in a roughly one-in-three chance of a quarter-point cut going into the meeting, took the hold in stride. The S&P 500 added 0.4% in the hour after the statement, the dollar drifted lower against most majors, and the two-year Treasury yield slipped four basis points to 3.86%.

What the data shows

Goods disinflation — long the easy part of the story — has now largely played out. The action over the next two quarters will be in services, where shelter costs, after years of stickiness, are finally rolling over in line with what private rent indices have been signalling since late 2024.

We need to see a few more months of data line up before we move. The risks of cutting too early and the risks of waiting too long are now much closer to balance than they were a year ago. — Jerome Powell, May 7, 2026

Powell's framing — that the risks are roughly balanced — is itself the new news. For most of 2024 and 2025, the Committee's bias was unambiguously toward keeping policy tight. Wednesday's statement edits that posture for the first time, swapping "appropriately restrictive" for "appropriately calibrated." Small word, big signal.

The market's read

Fed funds futures now imply roughly 55 basis points of cuts by year-end, concentrated in the September and December meetings. That's about ten basis points more dovish than a week ago and broadly consistent with the median of the FOMC's own Summary of Economic Projections, which the Committee left unchanged.

For equities, the read-through is straightforward: a Fed that is no longer leaning hawkish, against a backdrop of resilient corporate earnings, removes one of the last overhangs on multiples. For credit, spreads continue to grind tighter — investment grade is back near the lows of the cycle, and high yield is not far behind.

The bond market is doing more of the work. The 10-year is now 35 basis points off its January peak, and the curve has steepened sharply in the past six weeks as the front end prices cuts and the long end prices a reflation that policymakers, for now, are content to let unfold.

Key takeaways

  • Rates held at 4.00–4.25%; third hold in a row.
  • April CPI at 2.4%; core at 2.7% — both within reach of target.
  • Statement language softened from "restrictive" to "calibrated."
  • Markets price ~55 bps of cuts by year-end, with September the most likely first move.
  • Powell explicitly described risks as "balanced" for the first time this cycle.

The next meeting is in mid-July. With two more inflation prints and one more jobs report in between, the Committee will have plenty of data to chew on. For now, the message is simple: the Fed is ready to cut, but not in a hurry, and not before the data tells it to.
